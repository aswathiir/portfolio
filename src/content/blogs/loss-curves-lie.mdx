# Your loss curve is not your eval

This year I trained a lot of models. Three times, the training metrics looked fine while the model was broken. Here's what happened each time, and the check that would have caught it sooner.

## 1. A falling loss hiding a vocabulary-mask bug (MoA-CAS)

In MoA-CAS I train small bottleneck adapters on a frozen IndicConformer backbone so it handles code-switched Hindi-English speech better. The loss went down nicely. The decoded text didn't get better.

The cause turned out to be a vocabulary-mask bug. The model was optimising, just not towards anything useful. What caught it was a **decode-validation gate**: actually decoding held-out audio during training and checking the transcripts, not only the loss. With that in place the adapters cut WER from 55.4% to 45.4% on a speaker-disjoint test set.

**Lesson:** for any sequence model, regularly decode a few samples and read them.

## 2. A reward with zero gradient (SAHAi)

SAHAi is an LLM tutor trained with GRPO to guide students without giving away the solution. Early on, training ran but nothing moved. When every rollout in a group gets the same reward, the group-relative advantage is zero, so there's no gradient.

Reading the transcripts also showed role inversion (the tutor started acting like the student) and turns that were cut off mid-sentence. Switching to a **graded reward** raised reward spread from 0 to 1.32 and brought truncated turns down to about 5%.

**Lesson:** for RL, track reward *variance* as well as reward mean, and read the transcripts.

## 3. One wrong token ID (banking voice assistant)

During my internship, the IndicTrans2 translations in our multilingual voice assistant were quietly wrong. Nothing crashed. The outputs were just wrong. The cause was an incorrect `forced_bos_token_id` at inference time. After fixing it, translation accuracy came back in all four languages.

**Lesson:** test inference in isolation with known input/output pairs, before it's buried inside a pipeline.

---

The common thread: each time the training metric was technically correct and practically useless. Since then, I set up evaluation before I start training.
